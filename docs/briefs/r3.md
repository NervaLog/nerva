## Goal

Botpike's operator can read, from the README, how much memory a replica needs for a working set
and a choice of caches, and what p50, p99 and p99.9 a submit sees, measured on three replicas on
three machines with their own disks and the load and its gateway on a fourth. The p99 tail has a
named cause. A replica whose caches do not fit the memory it can see refuses to start, naming the
flag, instead of being killed later.

## Non-goals

- **Making Nerva faster.** R3 measures and explains. A change the numbers suggest (the commit
  stall's default, P136; batching, W5; the native client, R8) is its own decision and pull
  request. **Changing a cache default** is the owner's decision after the table (owner answer
  4), not a slice.
- **A nightly on the topology.** The nightly stays on one Machine (N56); R3's runs are by hand
  (`workflow_dispatch`). A nightly on three hosts is later, once the host is chosen for good.
- **Fly-only features** (P359b): no 6PN, no `flyctl` in the R3 harness. `perf/fly/` and
  `perf/nightly/` stay as they are, and R3 takes no Fly data point (P406e).
- **Provisioning from the repository** (Terraform, cloud CLIs, credentials for a cloud account).
  The owner provisions the hosts by hand from slice 4's list; the harness reaches them over SSH.
- **More than one region, more than three replicas, TLS on the gateway.**
- **Refusals in other commands.** Only `nerva start` checks its memory; `import` (1.2 GiB, README,
  "Upgrading a cluster") and `nerva api` are recorded in the sizing table, not checked.
- **`perf` profiles as a deliverable.** `perf_event_open` may work on the hosts; a profile is
  evidence for the p99 decision if it helps, never a gate.

## What exists today

**Every number ran on one host.** `perf/nightly/run.sh` starts three replicas on `127.0.0.1` on
one Fly Machine (performance-8x, 16 GB, one 50 GB volume; N56) and drives them with `bench-load`
there. N56 found that the three replicas' Direct IO on one shared volume dominates its load
numbers; #134 found that, with 8 clients, about 94% of an op's time is the primary's 10 ms commit
stall (P136), and that an op alone costs about 0.7 ms, bound by `O_DSYNC` WAL writes. Nobody has
measured replicas on separate machines with their own disks and a real network between them.

**The Fly tail.** PRODUCTION.md: the nightly shows p99 of 147 to 188 ms against 20 to 30 ms
locally, and nobody knows why. Known contributors, none measured on the topology: the stall
(10 ms ticks), the WAL's `O_DSYNC` writes, checkpoints (`commit_checkpoint_data` and
`commit_checkpoint_superblock`, logged at info, `src/vsr/replica.zig:5205`, `:5392`), compaction
(`tb.compact_*` in statsd) and the volume.

**The load generator** (`src/bench_load.zig`, N55, P132). One thread and one blocking client per
`--clients`, closed or open loop (`--rate`, direct workloads only: `botpike` refuses it, "botpike
is a closed loop: no --rate"), one JSON object out with ops/s, requests/s and latency p50, p90,
p99, p99.9 and max in µs (`result_write`) for the whole run, warm-up included (there is no
warm-up). No timeline: a tail cannot be placed in time. The `botpike` workload starts its own
`nerva api` (`--nerva`) beside the load and connects it to `--addresses`, which is already
P147g's sidecar, but with no `--statsd`, its stderr ignored and its PID not exposed
(`Gateway.start` in `src/bench_botpike.zig`); its setup declares the layouts and funds `--keys`
accounts (2 to 1,000,000), 255 per commit (`src/bench_botpike.zig:37`).

**Memory.** A replica allocates everything at start and logs it: `allocated <n>MiB during replica
init` (`command_start`, from `vsr.CountingAllocator`). At the defaults that is 2,925 MiB and
an RSS of about 3.0 GB (#127; README's Memory item): `--cache-grid` defaults to 1 GiB
(`src/config.zig:129`), `--cache-rows` to its minimum, one set
(`src/nerva/state_machine.zig:142`). The allocator under the counting one is a
`GeneralPurposeAllocator` over `stdx.huge_page_allocator` (`run` in `nerva_main.zig`), and the
`Tracer`, `IO` and `Storage` are allocated from it before `command_start`, so neither page
rounding nor those are counted.
Botpike's replicas run on 4 GB Machines (performance-1x, Botpike/app `fly/botpike-nerva.toml:99`)
beside a gateway, `socat` and a statsd exporter, at the defaults. The sizing decision #127 left
open is still open.

**Nothing checks memory at start.** `command_start` opens the replica,
which allocates and touches its caches; a replica whose caches exceed its cgroup's `memory.max` or
the machine is OOM-killed, during `open` or later, with no message of Nerva's. Nothing reads
`/proc/meminfo` or the cgroup today. The only budget allocator is the gateway's per-request
`MemoryBudget` (`src/nerva/api/memory_budget.zig`, #31, #60): it counts bytes and refuses, before
asking its parent, an allocation past `limit`.

**What `Replica.open` writes, and when** (`src/vsr/replica.zig`, `Replica.open`): it calls
`SuperBlock.init`, then `superblock.open`, then `self.init` (the large allocations: grid cache,
forest, journal, clients), then disables allocation, then `journal.recover`. `SuperBlock.open`
**can write**: when a copy of the superblock is damaged, it repairs it (`repair`, then
`write_header`, `storage.write_sectors` on the superblock zone) with the working superblock, before
`init`. Nothing else writes before `init` returns: WAL and grid IO start with `journal.recover`.

**Metrics.** Replicas send statsd every 10 s (`--statsd`, N50), including `tb.replica_commit_us`
by stage (`stall`, `compact`, `checkpoint_*`), `tb.storage_*_us` by zone and
`tb.grid_cache_hits`/`misses`; gateways send `nerva.api.*` (R4). `--trace` writes a Perfetto
trace and costs 15 to 25% throughput (#134).

**The host.** P359b and Botpike/app P161 (2026-11-12): plan for generic Linux VMs, most likely
Azure first; the choice is "a later decision, with costs". Botpike's ledger runs on three Fly
Machines today and stays there until something replaces it. The nightly's Fly app `nerva-perf`
and its secret `FLY_API_TOKEN_PERF` exist.

## Design

### The topology: 3+1

Four hosts: `replica0`, `replica1`, `replica2`, each running one `nerva start` on its own data
disk, and `load`, running `bench-load`, the `nerva api` gateway `bench-load` starts beside it
(P147g: the gateway lives with its client), and the statsd sink. Replicas run production flags
(no `--development`), a fresh random cluster key per run (as `perf/nightly/run.sh` does), and
`--statsd=<load>:8125`; the gateway gets the same through `bench-load --gateway-statsd`
(slice 1). The harness is the same whether the four "hosts" are four machines over SSH or four
directories on one machine (`--hosts=local`, for CI and sessions). Local mode checks the harness,
never the hosts: it runs only the preflight checks that mean something on one machine, and a
local run never counts toward delivery (P406b, P406f).

### The harness: `perf/topology/` (P406)

Python 3, stdlib only, like `perf/nightly/compare.py` and the fleet's reporter (P147i).

- `run.py --topology 3+1 --hosts=<inventory>|local --nerva=<path> --bench-load=<path>
  --output=<dir> [--sizing [--memory-only]] [--seconds=<n>] [--warmup=<n>] [--keys=<n>]`, and
  `run.py --clean --hosts=…` alone.
- **The inventory** (`hosts.txt`, one line per host): role, SSH target, private address, and for
  a replica its data disk's provisioned IOPS and MB/s (the report prints them). The
  SSH target may name a jump host (`ProxyJump`); host keys are pinned in a `known_hosts` the
  workflow writes from a secret. The harness never runs `sudo`.
- **Per host, before anything starts** (`preflight`): kernel, `nproc`, `MemTotal`, the data
  disk's file system and mount options, free space, that `nerva version` runs, and a 20 s `fio`
  4 KiB `O_DIRECT` random-write as the nightly's `health()`; between hosts, 20 pings from `load`
  to each replica and from each replica to the next (RTT p50 and max). Any failure stops the run
  before a replica starts.
- **Preflight with `--hosts=local`** (P406f): one machine, so it runs only what makes sense
  there: kernel, `nproc`, `MemTotal`, the data directory's file system, mount options and free
  space, and `nerva version`. It does not run `fio`, the clock check or the pings, and records
  each as `skipped` in the preflight facts and in `report.md` (`fio: skipped (local)`, `clock:
  skipped (local: one clock)`, `rtt: skipped (local)`), whether or not the tools are installed:
  CI's runner and session containers have no `fio`, `chronyc` or `ping`, and a container's PID 1
  is not systemd. So local mode needs nothing installed beyond Python 3, and its report is never
  delivery evidence.
- **Per run:** copy the binaries (SHA-256 checked on each host), format three data files,
  start the replicas with stderr piped through a stamper (`node.py`, one per host: each line
  prefixed with wall-clock µs, `CLOCK_REALTIME`), wait for the cluster to commit, run the
  workloads (`bench-load` with `--gateway-statsd=<load>:8125 --gateway-pid-file=<file>
  --gateway-stderr=inherit`, its stderr piped through `node.py` as the replicas' are, so the
  gateway's log is stamped too), collect, stop and delete the data files. A trap tears everything down on failure or interrupt, and
  `run.py --clean` removes what a killed run left.
- **Workloads** (closed loop unless said; `bench-load --warmup=30` (slice 1: the warm-up's
  operations are left out of the summary histogram and its timeline lines are flagged), then
  `--seconds`, default 300):

  | Key | Command (on `load`) | Why |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `botpike/c1` | `bench-load --workload=botpike --clients=1 --nerva=…` | One caller: the floor |
  | `botpike/c12` | the same, `--clients=12` | Botpike's 12 in flight (P99b) |
  | `c1/r100` | `--workload=c1 --clients=8 --rate=100` | Open loop, direct: the tail at a load that is not saturating (`botpike` is closed loop only, and R3 does not add an open loop to it) |
  | `c4/c8` | `--workload=c4 --clients=8` | Lookups, direct |
  | `c1/c8` | `--workload=c1 --clients=8` | Puts, direct |

- **What it records:** `bench-load`'s JSON and its timeline (slice 1); every replica's stamped
  log and the gateway's; every statsd line, stamped at arrival; each replica's and the gateway's
  (its PID from `--gateway-pid-file`) `VmRSS` and `VmHWM` at start, after setup and at the end,
  and each replica's `allocated <n>MiB` line; the preflight facts.
- **The report** (`report.py`): `report.md` and `results.json`. One row per workload: ops/s, p50,
  p99, p99.9, max, and **p99 of the seconds with a checkpoint** against **p99 of the seconds
  without** (a second is "with" when any replica logged `checkpoint_data start` or
  `checkpoint_superblock start` in it), each computed by merging those seconds' histogram buckets
  from the timeline (never by averaging per-second p99s), the share of `tb.replica_commit_us`
  spent in `stall`, and the host facts. Warm-up seconds are left out of both.
- **Clocks:** marks from four hosts are compared at 1 s granularity, so each host's offset must be
  well under that; `preflight` records `chronyc tracking`'s offset (or `timedatectl`'s sync flag)
  and refuses a host more than 10 ms off. Local mode has one clock and skips this (above).

### Two runs agree (P406c)

`same.py run1/ run2/` compares two runs of the same commit: ops/s, p50 and p99 of every workload
within **10%** (PRODUCTION's bound); p99.9 and max are printed beside them and not checked (at
about 150 submits/s for 300 s, p99.9 rests on about 45 samples); memory within **1%**. It prints
one line per number and ends `same: PASS` or `same: FAIL <key> <metric> <a> <b>`.

- **Which memory numbers** (P406f): only each replica's `allocated <n>MiB` line, which is fixed at
  start and in practice equal between runs. `VmRSS` and `VmHWM` (every sample, replicas and
  gateway) are printed beside it and not checked: RSS moves with THP and the kernel, and the
  gateway's is tens of MiB, so 1% is under 1 MiB and its arena alone moves more.

- **Within 10%** is `|a − b| / min(a, b) ≤ 0.10`, strict and symmetric (a = 100, b = 110 passes;
  b = 110.5 fails). Memory is the same formula at 0.01.
- **Histogram resolution.** p50 and p99 are `LatencyHistogram` bucket upper bounds
  (`sub_bucket_count = 16`): adjacent buckets above 16 µs are 1/32 to 1/16 apart (3.1 to 6.25%).
  So one bucket's difference always passes and two may not; a real 4% change can read as one or two
  buckets. `same.py` prints each value's bucket width beside it, and the bound is not widened for
  it.
- **Which runs count.** The two runs of one `perf-r3.yml` dispatch with `runs=2`, back to back on
  the same four VMs: they measure minutes-apart noise on those VMs, not VM-to-VM or day-to-day
  variance, and the README says so beside the numbers. The **first** such dispatch on the
  delivered commit is the one that counts; `docs/demos/r3.md` lists every `perf-r3` dispatch on
  that commit, failures included. A later dispatch counts only if the owner waives the first in
  writing (a decision), never by re-dispatching until one passes.

### The sizing table (`--sizing`)

A sweep on the same hosts: for each cell, format, start the three replicas with the cell's caches,
run `botpike/c12` and `c4/c8` for 120 s each after setup, record, stop.

| Dimension | Values |
| --- | --- |
| `--cache-grid` | 512 MiB, 1 GiB (default), 2 GiB |
| `--cache-rows` | the minimum (default), 256 MiB |
| Working set (`--keys`, accounts) | 100,000 and 1,000,000 |

Twelve cells, about an hour. Per cell: the `allocated` line and RSS of a replica, the gateway's
RSS, grid cache hit ratio (`tb.grid_cache_hits / (hits + misses)`), storage reads per op
(`tb.storage_read_us` count / committed ops), p50 and p99 of both workloads. The README table
(slice 7) has one row per cache setting with its memory, and per working set the hit ratio,
reads and p99, then a line "a machine for one replica and its neighbours needs replica +
gateway(s) + exporter + 25% headroom" (test plan 0.6.0, item 5), from which Botpike's size
follows.

Memory does not depend on the host: allocation is fixed at start. So `--sizing --memory-only
--hosts=local` measures the memory columns anywhere, and `demo-r3` checks them against the README
on every push, so the table cannot rot. `--memory-only` runs the six cache settings (the working
set does not change the allocation), and per setting starts **one** replica (formatted, with the
three-member `--addresses`), waits for its `allocated` line, samples its `VmRSS` then, and stops
it before the next starts: one replica at a time, so the most live at once is the largest
setting's one replica, about 4.2 GiB (2,925 MiB at the defaults, plus 1 GiB more grid and 256 MiB
of rows), which fits a 16 GB runner. It holds the
`allocated` value within 1% of the README's, and RSS only to `allocated ≤ RSS ≤ allocated +
memory_reserve` (RSS moves with THP and the kernel; the reserve is what this brief claims it
covers).

### A replica that does not fit refuses to start (P406d)

- **What it can see** (`src/nerva/start_memory.zig`, new; not `memory_budget.zig`, which is the
  gateway's): the smallest of `MemTotal` (`/proc/meminfo`) and every cgroup limit on the path
  from the process's cgroup up to the root, since a limit is often on an ancestor (a systemd
  slice, a pod above its container):
  - v2: `/proc/self/cgroup`'s `0::<path>`; read `/sys/fs/cgroup/<path>/memory.max`, then each
    parent's, up to the mount root; `max` means none at that level.
  - v1: the `memory` controller's line (`N:memory:<path>`, or a line whose controllers include
    `memory`) under `/sys/fs/cgroup/memory/`, `memory.limit_in_bytes` at each level; a value of
    at least 2^62 (v1's "unlimited", for example 9223372036854771712) means none.
  - If `<path>` is not under the mount (a container without a cgroup namespace), it reads the
    mount root's limit alone.
  - At most `cgroup_depth_max` (32) levels, asserted. A file that is absent counts as no limit at
    that level; one that cannot be parsed is a refusal (NRV-E0723 names the file), never a guess.
- **The budget:** what it can see minus `memory_reserve` (256 MiB). The reserve covers what the
  counting allocator does not see: the binary, stacks and page tables; the `Tracer`, `IO` and
  `Storage`, allocated in `run` before `command_start`; and the base allocator's page rounding
  and THP (a `GeneralPurposeAllocator` over `stdx.huge_page_allocator`). If what it can see is
  256 MiB or less, it refuses at once.
- **The check:** `command_start` puts the gateway's existing `MemoryBudget`
  (`src/nerva/api/memory_budget.zig`, reused as is, `request_start` never called: a one-shot
  budget) with the budget as `limit` between its base allocator and the vendored
  `CountingAllocator` (itself unchanged; the wrap is a named patch to `src/nerva_main.zig`,
  below). It refuses an allocation that would take the live
  total past the limit **before** asking its parent, so nothing past the budget is ever touched.
  `error.OutOfMemory` from `MessagePool.init` or `Replica.open`, with `exceeded` set, becomes:

  ```
  error[NRV-E0723]: start: the caches do not fit: this replica may use 2816 MiB (cgroup memory.max
  3072 MiB at /system.slice/run-r1.scope, less 256 MiB), and needs more than that with
  --cache-grid=1GiB --cache-rows=<min>
    fix: lower --cache-grid or --cache-rows (README, "Memory and caches", gives each setting's
    total), or give the machine or its cgroup more memory
  ```

  exit 1, blame operator. The message names no value to lower to: the replica does not know its
  total need when it is refused, and a guessed value could still not fit.
- **What may be written before the refusal.** The budget can refuse anywhere up to the end of
  `Replica.open`'s `init` (above, "What `Replica.open` writes, and when"). The only write before
  that is `SuperBlock.open`'s repair of a damaged superblock copy: it writes the working
  superblock, the one every later open reads, to the damaged copies; it is idempotent and leaves
  no half-opened file. That is accepted: the criterion is **no write before the last allocation
  other than superblock-copy repair**. No WAL or grid write happens before `init` returns. Slice 2
  shows it by citing the lines, and by a test (below) with one damaged copy.
- **Not in `prefetch` or `commit`:** startup only. The state machine's determinism is untouched.
- **The vendored patch** (named here, so that slice 2 does not stop on CLAUDE.md's stop item 2):
  `src/nerva_main.zig` is a vendored file with a local-patch row in `UPSTREAM.md` (the `version`
  row, P284b, extended by #271). Slice 2 patches `command_start` only: read the budget from
  `start_memory.zig`, wrap the base allocator in `MemoryBudget` under the `CountingAllocator`,
  and map `error.OutOfMemory` with `exceeded` set to NRV-E0723. Nothing else in the file changes.
  In the same commit it extends that row with one sentence: "`command_start` puts a one-shot
  `MemoryBudget` (`src/nerva/api/memory_budget.zig`, its limit from
  `src/nerva/start_memory.zig`) between the base allocator and the `CountingAllocator`, and
  reports an allocation it refuses as NRV-E0723 (R3 slice 2, P406d)."

### The tail, explained

On the topology, from `botpike/c12`, `c1/r100` and `c4/c8`:
1. **Place it:** p99 with and without checkpoint seconds; statsd windows of high p99 against
   `compact`, `checkpoint_*`, `stall` and `tb.storage_write_us` in the same windows; ping RTT.
2. **Take one cause away at a time**, one 300 s run each: `--commit-stall-probability=0` (P136;
   its lag cost is recorded too, from "lagging behind" log lines); `--cache-grid=2GiB`; the data
   files on a RAM disk (`tmpfs` with `--development`: IO removed, labelled "not production").
3. **One traced run** (`--trace` on all three replicas, 60 s, `--trace-size-max=1GiB`) for the
   shape of the slowest seconds.
4. **The decision** (slice 7) names the cause, with the numbers, and says what would remove it.

R3 takes no data point on Fly (P406e): the Fly tail is explained on the topology alone.

### `bench-load`: `--timeline`, `--warmup` and the gateway's flags (slice 1)

- `--timeline=<path>`: one line a second, JSON:
  `{"t_us":…,"count":…,"p50_us":…,"p99_us":…,"max_us":…,"errors":…,"buckets":[[i,n],…]}`,
  from a per-second `LatencyHistogram` beside the run's. `buckets` lists the second's non-zero
  buckets (index, count), so `report.py` can merge any set of seconds into one histogram.
  `t_us` is wall-clock (`CLOCK_REALTIME`) µs at the second's start, comparable with `node.py`'s
  stamps, not the monotonic clock `bench-load` times with. An operation belongs to the second in
  which it **ended**. Open loop latencies count from the due time, as today.
- `--warmup=<s>` (default 0): the first `<s>` seconds run as usual, their operations are left
  out of the summary JSON and its histogram, and their timeline lines carry `"warmup":true`.
  `--warmup` + `--seconds` is at most `seconds_max` (3,600), asserted, so the timeline
  never has more lines than that.
- `--gateway-statsd=<host:port>` (botpike only): passed to the gateway as `nerva api --statsd`.
- `--gateway-pid-file=<path>` (botpike only): the gateway's PID, written right after it spawns,
  so the harness samples its RSS as it does a replica's.
- `--gateway-stderr=inherit|ignore` (botpike only; default `ignore`, as today): `inherit` passes
  the gateway's stderr through to `bench-load`'s, which the harness stamps; the gateway's lines
  are told apart by their log scope.

## Bounds

| Constant | Value | Where | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `seconds_max` | 3,600 | `bench_timeline.zig` | One line a second for an hour; `--warmup` + `--seconds` above it is refused |
| `memory_reserve` | 256 MiB | `start_memory.zig` | The process beyond counted allocations (binary about 20 MiB, stacks, the `Tracer`, `IO` and `Storage`, page rounding and THP, page tables), with margin |
| Proc and cgroup files read | at most 4 KiB each | `start_memory.zig` | One page; longer is a refusal |
| `cgroup_depth_max` | 32 levels | `start_memory.zig` | The walk from the process's cgroup to the root; deeper is a refusal |
| Workload seconds | 30 to 3,600 less the warm-up (3,570 at the default warm-up of 30), default 300 | `run.py` | 300 s gives about 45,000 submits at 12 callers; warm-up + seconds stays within `seconds_max` |
| Clock offset | 10 ms | `run.py` preflight | Marks are compared at 1 s |
| Statsd lines kept | 200,000 per run | `run.py` sink | About 3 hours of four processes; past it the run fails, never drops silently |
| A whole run | 4 hours | `perf-r3.yml` | Two runs and the sweep take about 2.5 hours |
| `demo_r3` | 30 minutes | `ci.yml` | Two local runs (five workloads at 5 s warm-up + 30 s, and setup) about 10 minutes, the memory sweep about 1 |

Each gets a test at the bound and one past it: `--warmup=30 --seconds=3570` and `--seconds=3571`
(and `bench-load --seconds=3600` and `3601`); a cgroup at exactly budget + 256 MiB and one byte
under; a 4,097-byte `memory.max`; 32 cgroup levels and 33.

## New error codes

Registered in `docs/ERRORS.md` by slice 2 (E0721 and E0722 are taken by open #333):

| Code | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| NRV-E0723 | The replica's caches do not fit the memory it may use (the machine's, or the tightest cgroup limit above it, less 256 MiB): exit 1, nothing written to the data file but, at most, the repair of a damaged superblock copy |

## Risks

1. **The host is not chosen.** Measuring on a host Botpike then leaves gives the right memory
   table (host-independent) and the wrong latencies. The harness is generic, so a re-run on the
   chosen host is one dispatch; the README says which host its latencies come from.
2. **Two runs may not agree within 10% at p99** on shared cloud disks and networks. If they do
   not, the finding is the noise itself: the report names its source (a host, the disk, the
   network), and the owner chooses dedicated hosts or a wider bound (owner's call), never a
   re-run until it passes.
3. **The budget refuses inside vendored `open`.** `SuperBlock.open` may already have repaired a
   damaged superblock copy by then (above); that write is idempotent and accepted. Any other
   write before `init` returns would risk a half-opened file; slice 2 cites the code and stops if
   it finds one. A pre-flight estimate from the flags was rejected: the fixed part of the
   allocation moves with every release.
4. **RSS is not allocation.** The reserve covers the difference measured today (about 10 MiB at
   the defaults: #127's startup RSS of 3,005,708 kB is 2,935 MiB, against `allocated 2925MiB`),
   and what the budget cannot see: the `Tracer`, `IO`
   and `Storage` allocated before `command_start`, and the huge-page allocator's rounding and THP.
   The sweep records both; `demo-r3` holds RSS to at most `allocated` + the reserve, and slice 7
   corrects the reserve if the hosts show more.
5. **SSH open to the internet** on `load`: keys only, a dedicated user without `sudo`, a key used
   for nothing else; the replicas have no public address. The VMs hold no data of anyone's.
6. **The demo on a shared runner** checks the harness and memory, not latency. A latency
   regression is the nightly's job (N56), not R3's.
7. **`bench-load` measures the gateway and the cluster together** for `botpike` (P132): a slow
   gateway hides a faster cluster. The direct workloads (`c4`, `c1`) stay for that.
