## Goal

An operator declares how long Nerva keeps three kinds of past state: commit keys, row and record
history, and view and group versions. The cluster trims each kind below its horizon,
deterministically, as part of the log, with a bounded amount of work per op. What the cluster
holds is then bounded by its **live set** plus one horizon of the past and the trimming backlog
(owner question 9):
- **Kept whatever the horizon:** every row's and record's current value, every tombstone, and
  each row's, record's, group's and view's last change or version at the horizon (keep-last).
  This part grows with the number of rows and records ever created: a 128-byte row and its
  128-byte change each, or a 512-byte record and its 512-byte record change, with their index
  entries. R7 does not bound it.
- **Bounded by the horizons:** every older change and version, and every commit key, which go
  once they are below their horizon. This part is one horizon's worth of writes, plus what
  trimming has not yet reached.

So a load that rewrites a fixed set of rows and records holds a flat amount of data: the data
file's live blocks, the export and its time, and `nerva view`'s memory stay flat. A load that
creates new rows or records grows by those rows and their last changes, linearly, and R7 says so
rather than hiding it.

**Cut: retention of facts.** GOALS-1.0 goal 7 lists "retention of keys and facts" as not yet
done. R7 delivers keys, and history and versions; the first client's facts (executions,
receipts, runs: P123b) are new records per operation, so R7 does not bound their growth. This
brief cuts facts' retention to a later milestone, and the decision that records the owner's
answer amends GOALS-1.0's line (owner question 8). `docs/demos/r7.md` records the cut.

What was trimmed is refused by name:
- a resent key older than its horizon answers `key_expired`, never `committed`;
- a read of the past below a horizon answers `before_horizon`, never a wrong answer.

## The operator's story

1. Today a cluster grows without bound:
   - each keyed transaction leaves an 80-byte key row and a change, forever (P123);
   - each write leaves a 128-byte change (512 for a record);
   - each view or group write leaves a version.

   An export of a year-old cluster replays a year.
2. The operator runs `nerva api` and sends:

   ```
   PUT /v1/retention {"keys": "7d", "history": "30d", "versions": "30d"}
   ```

   The declaration commits like any other op; `GET /v1/retention` shows it with each kind's
   `horizon_op`, the feed's trimmed timestamp, and how far trimming has caught up.
3. From then on the primary's pulses trim, a bounded slice per pulse, until each kind is caught
   up, and then keep up. Writes go on; a pulse is one op among them.
4. A client resends a key from 8 days ago: `409 key_expired` (NRV-E0213). It does not apply. The
   client knows the outcome is unknowable from Nerva and goes to its own records.
5. A client asks for a row as of 40 days ago, or for changes after a cursor from 40 days ago:
   `410 before_horizon` (NRV-E0411), naming the horizon. `nerva view` and `nerva export` read the
   compacted prefix instead (see "The feed below the horizon").
6. Loosening a horizon keeps what is still there: trimmed data does not come back, and a key
   once trimmed stays refused, even with `keys` redeclared as forever. Tightening it trims more,
   at the same bounded pace.

## Non-goals

- **Shrinking the data file.** Trimmed blocks return to the free set and are reused; the file
  never shrinks (upstream's grid has no truncation). "Flat" is measured as blocks acquired, not
  file size.
- **Reclaiming tombstones** (N3): a deleted row's tombstone, and a row's current value, are kept
  whatever the horizon. So are the catalog, layouts, ledgers, views' and groups' current state,
  members, indexes and their entries.
- **Retention of facts**, of rows or records themselves, by age or otherwise: cut (see "Goal",
  owner question 8). A row or record that is never deleted is kept, with its last change.
- **Per-table or per-view horizons.** One horizon per kind, cluster-wide. A later milestone may add
  them; the declaration's layout keeps reserved bytes for it.
- **Horizons by op count** or by size. Time only, from prepare timestamps.
- **Object storage, tiering, archiving** what is trimmed. An operator who must keep everything
  exports before trimming, or does not declare a horizon.
- **Trimming at compaction time.** It would never fire: append-only, timestamp-keyed tables reach
  the last level by `move_table` and are never rewritten there (`compaction.zig`). See "Design".
  This departs from R7's scope in `docs/PRODUCTION.md` ("Compaction drops what is older,
  deterministically"): here pulses write tombstones and compaction only reclaims them. The brief
  does not edit that line; it is owner question 7, and the answer's pull request amends it.
- **Trimming inside client ops.** Each tree's tables are sized at comptime for one op's writes
  (`groove.zig:323`, asserted at `tree.zig:117`); a client op that also trimmed could exceed
  them, and raising them changes the table layout. Only pulses trim.
- **A rolling upgrade into R7's release.** Its formats move the digest; P259c says export and
  import (see "Formats and R6").

## What exists today

- **Nothing is ever removed.**
  - Rows are deleted by tombstone (N3); `nerva.zig:115` says "Rows are never physically
    removed".
  - Changes (`tree_ids` 3, 4), group versions (12, 13), view versions (14, 15) and record
    changes (20, 21) are append-only grooves with `objects_cache = false`.
  - Commit keys are rows of reserved table `0xFFFF_0003`, written once at `commit_key_id(key)`
    (the first 8 bytes of SHA-256 of `"nerva.commit_key.v1"` and the key). The value is
    `CommitKey {key, checksum, entry_count, reserved[44]}`, 80 bytes, and it is also a change
    in the feed (P123). `row_put` writes the row and its change with the same timestamp.
  - `commit_key_checksum` covers the transaction's entries after the key entry, not the key
    entry itself.
- **Removal in the vendored groove is unfinished.**
  - `Groove.remove(key)` (`src/lsm/groove.zig:1879`) asserts `constants.verify`, requires an
    objects cache, and carries upstream's "TODO: Nothing currently calls or tests this method".
  - Cacheless grooves cannot remove at all.
  - UPSTREAM.md records that `forest_fuzz` was dropped "until Nerva's forest has real grooves".
- **Tombstones are dropped by compaction** at the last level, or when no lower level can hold the
  key (`compaction.zig:706-719`). So a removal that writes a tombstone per object is reclaimed by
  ordinary compaction, and the space returns to the free set.
- **Each tree's per-op limit is a layout.** A groove's `batch_value_count_max` per tree is the
  most values one op may put in it (`commit_entries_max` for trees 1 to 4); tables hold
  `lsm_compaction_ops` (32) times that (`groove.zig:323`), comptime. A tombstone counts as a value.
- **Pulses exist.**
  - The primary prepares `pulse` from `on_pulse_timeout` every `pulse_timeout` (100 ms), unless
    the prepare pipeline is full, and after any commit while `pulse_needed` is true
    (`replica.zig:5110`).
  - That pulse is a request to itself, pushed at the **tail** of the request queue
    (`push_request`, `replica.zig:12545`), behind client requests already queued.
  - `pulse_enabled` is false while a pulse is in the pipeline or the queue, so at most one pulse
    is ever in flight.
  - `pulse_needed` (`state_machine.zig:1155`) is true while a view or an index is building
    (N52). Backfill already shows the pattern R7 needs: a cursor in a singleton groove, a
    bounded batch per pulse, all reads in `prefetch`.
- **Scans are serial.** `prefetch` acquires one scan buffer at a time and resets it in its
  callback; `lsm_scans_max` is 6.
- **The format digest** (R6 slice 1, `src/nerva/format_digest.zig`) hashes layouts, `tree_ids`,
  the cluster config, the vendored layouts and the derivations, with `format_epoch = 1`.
  - R6's later slices are not merged: `nerva_main.zig:750` is `.single_release`, and
    `build.zig:25` has `release_bundled = null`.
- **The export file** is version 2 at most (`export_file.zig:29`). It replays every op from 1
  (N80); import refuses a delete of a row that is not live (N81). Backups record the export
  version in their manifest (`backup.zig:206`).
- **The feed.** `get_changes` takes `ChangesFilter {timestamp_min, limit, reserved[52]}`
  (`nerva.zig:937`) and returns changes with `timestamp ≥ timestamp_min` (0: from the
  beginning). It has no op cursor. The gateway's `/v1/changes` already leaves key rows out.
- **Statuses.** `EntryStatus` runs to `id_collision = 24` (`format_test.zig:105`); new values are
  appended from 25.
- **The free-set gauge.** `grid_blocks_acquired` exists (`src/trace/event.zig:591`), which is
  the demo's measure.

## Design

### Declared in the log

- **Operations.** A new `Operation.declare_retention` (the next free reserved value, appended)
  carries:

  ```
  Retention {keys_s, history_s, versions_s: u64, reserved}
  ```

  Each value is a horizon in seconds; 0 means forever. A new `get_retention` reads it.
- **Declaring is changeable.** Unlike a view, retention may be declared again, and the latest
  declaration holds.
- **Bounds on a declaration.** A horizon is 0 or between `retention_min` and `retention_max`;
  otherwise NRV-E0310.
- **The default is forever.** A cluster that never declares trims nothing and behaves exactly as
  today, apart from the new trees, which stay empty, and the predecessor links ("Predecessor
  links"), which every write now sets.
- **The retention state is a singleton groove** (new `tree_ids` 27 and 28, following the
  backfill cursor's pattern), not a reserved-table row, which would add a change to the feed on
  every pulse.

### Tracks and the horizon

Trimming walks five **tracks**, one per trimmed tree, each in timestamp order:

| Track | Kind | Walks | Removes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| changes | history | tree 3 (changes) | a change's predecessor (trees 3, 4) |
| record changes | history | tree 20 | a record change's predecessor (trees 20, 21) |
| group versions | versions | tree 12 | a version's predecessor (trees 12, 13) |
| view versions | versions | tree 14 | a version's predecessor (trees 14, 15) |
| keys | keys | tree 1 (rows), skipping rows not in `0xFFFF0003` | the key row and its change (trees 1, 2, 3, 4) |

The keys track walks rows, not changes: tree 1 is in timestamp order, and a key row's timestamp
is its change's, so removing the row gives the change's timestamp with no read.

The retention groove holds the declared horizons and, per track:
- `cursor_ts`: the timestamp of the newest object examined (every object at or below it has
  been examined);
- `cursor_op`: that object's op;
- `trimmed_ts`: the newest timestamp removed (0: nothing yet);
- `next_ts`: a lower bound on the oldest object not yet examined.

**The horizon of a kind is an op:** `horizon_op` is the largest `cursor_op` of the kind's
tracks, 0 before anything was examined. It moves whenever the cursor does, even where an
examination removed nothing, so it is conservative but never wrong:
- **every as-of read at an op ≥ `horizon_op` is exact** ("What stays exact", below);
- **every as-of read at an op < `horizon_op` answers `before_horizon`**, whether or not the
  answer it would have given was removed.

The feed has no op cursor; it is refused by timestamp ("The feed below the horizon").

### Trimming by pulses

- **When a pulse is needed.** `pulse_needed` is also true when, for some track of a kind with a
  horizon, `next_ts ≤ now − horizon`. `now` is the prepare timestamp; there is no clock.
  - A pulse's scan that reaches the end of its tree sets `next_ts` to the pulse's prepare
    timestamp + 1: anything written later is newer.
  - A scan that stops at an object above the horizon sets `next_ts` to that object's timestamp.
  - A scan that stops at its budget sets `next_ts` to `cursor_ts + 1`, still due.

  So a caught-up, idle cluster pulses for retention at most once per horizon per track, and
  `pulse_needed` is false between those times. It is never true forever once a horizon is
  declared.
- **One scan per track per pulse.** `prefetch` runs at most one forward range scan per track,
  from `cursor_ts + 1` up to `now − horizon`, limited to the track's budget. The scans run one
  after another, as backfill's do, so they need one scan at a time, within `lsm_scans_max`.
  What a scan returns is kept in a buffer per track, allocated at startup and sized to the
  track's budget, for `commit`. Nothing else is read: removal needs no predecessor read
  ("Predecessor links").
- **`commit` removes** by writing tombstones from the scanned objects' own fields, then moves the
  cursor fields past what it examined. The removal is deterministic because it is a function of
  the log and the forest.
- **The budget is per tree, and fits its tables.** A pulse writes into each trimmed tree at most
  that tree's `batch_value_count_max`, the limit its tables are sized for (`groove.zig:323`):
  - a history or versions track examines at most its tree's limit, and each examined object
    removes at most one object from each of its two trees;
  - the keys track examines at most tree 1's limit, rows skipped included, and each key row
    removes one object from each of trees 1, 2, 3 and 4;
  - trees 3 and 4 are shared by the keys and changes tracks, which go one after the other within
    those trees' limits; which goes first alternates with the pulse's op parity, so neither
    starves;
  - a pulse that backfills (N52) does not trim a tree that backfill writes. Trimming of that tree
    waits until the backfill is done.

  So a pulse's removals in a tree never exceed what one client op may write there, and no table
  layout changes.
- **The lag.** See "The lag bound" below; it is owner question 9.
- **Compaction reclaims the space.** Tombstones on append-only, timestamp-keyed tables meet their
  object at the next compaction of that range and are dropped at the last level. Trimming adds
  ordinary compaction work, about one tombstone per removed object, which the existing pacing
  absorbs.
- **The vendored patch.** Removal needs a minimal patch to `groove.zig`:
  - `remove_by_key`, for cacheless grooves: tombstones in the objects tree by timestamp
    (`tombstone_from_key`) and in each index tree by key, given by the caller, since there is no
    cache to look the object up in;
  - `remove_object(object)`, for the rows groove, which also evicts the object from the cache;
  - `remove` enabled outside `verify` builds.

  It is recorded in UPSTREAM.md, with a groove fuzzer standing in for the dropped `forest_fuzz`
  (slice 2).

### The lag bound

How many client ops commit between two pulses decides whether trimming keeps up. Today, by the
code above:
- at most one pulse is in flight, and it is queued at the tail, behind every client request
  already queued or prepared;
- each client has at most one request in flight, so at most `clients_max` (64) client ops sit
  ahead of it;
- so between two pulses, at most q client ops commit, where q ≤ 64 is the number of clients with
  a request outstanding.

Per tree, a client op writes at most L objects (that tree's limit), and a pulse removes at most
L. So **trimming keeps pace when the client ops between two pulses write, on average, at most
L/q objects per tree that will later be trimmed.** With one writer (q = 1) it always keeps pace,
max-size batches included. Under sustained overload by many writers at the batch limit, the
backlog (objects below the horizon not yet examined) grows by up to (q − 1) × L per pulse, on
disk, and drains at up to L per pulse once the load falls. Nothing in memory grows: the
backlog is data on disk, and every queue and buffer keeps its asserted limit.

A hard bound needs one of three changes, and the choice is owner question 9:
- **(a) No change** (recommended): ship the bound above as stated, with W0704 as the alarm when
  a caught-up track falls more than a minute behind, and a demo step that overloads with many
  writers and asserts the backlog drains.
- **(b) Deterministic backpressure:** while any track's `next_ts` is more than `lag_max` behind
  its horizon, client ops that write to a trimmed tree are refused with a new status
  `retention_behind` (EntryStatus 27, its code taken when the answer lands). The backlog is
  then at most `lag_max` of writes plus `clients_max` × L. It costs a wire change, added to
  slice 1's formats, and refusals under load.
- **(c) A vendored patch to `src/vsr/replica.zig`** that pushes the pulse at the head of the
  request queue. That is a stop item (CLAUDE.md, item 2) unless the owner names the patch. It
  still lets the pipeline's `pipeline_prepare_queue_max` (8) client ops commit between two
  pulses, so the bound is (a)'s with q ≤ 8, not a hard one. Not recommended alone.

Raising each tree's per-op limit instead would change the table layout, a format stop, and is not
offered.

### History: the keep-last rule

- **The rule.** Every row keeps its newest change at or below the cursor, and every change above
  it. Since the cursor is at most `now − history_s`, every row keeps the change that was current
  at the horizon.
- **How a forward cursor applies it: trim the predecessor.** When the changes track examines a
  change C' of row R, it removes C, R's previous change, if there is one.
  - Checking C itself ("is a newer change of R below the horizon?") would not work with a forward
    cursor: when the cursor reaches C, R's next change is usually still above the horizon, and
    the cursor never comes back to C. Trimming the predecessor gives the same result and never
    looks back.
  - A row's first change has no predecessor and removes nothing. A deleted row's tombstone change
    is its last change, and the row itself is never touched, so it keeps both. A key row's
    change is a first change, so the changes track never removes it; only the keys track does.
- **The invariant.** Let c be the changes track's `cursor_ts`. For every row R:
  - exactly one of R's changes at or below c is kept, the newest; every change of R above c is
    kept.

  By induction on the changes the cursor examines: before R's first change, R has none at or
  below c. When the cursor examines C' of R, R's only kept change at or below the old cursor is,
  by the hypothesis, the newest one older than C', which is C' 's predecessor; removing it leaves
  C' as R's only kept change at or below the new cursor. Changes of other rows are untouched.
  Each change is removed at most once: only its successor's examination removes it.
- **What stays exact.** Let o ≥ `horizon_op`, and let D be R's newest change with op ≤ o (the
  answer to an as-of read of R at o). If D is above the cursor, it is kept. If D is at or below
  it, every change of R between D and the cursor has an op ≤ `cursor_op` ≤ o, so there is none
  (D is the newest with op ≤ o): D is R's newest change at or below the cursor, and is kept. And
  if R has no change with op ≤ o, none was removed that could have answered. So the read is
  exact. Rows, records and their tombstones are never touched.
- **What is refused.** An as-of read at an op below `horizon_op` answers `before_horizon`: an end
  record with a new flag bit, then HTTP 410 at the gateway with NRV-E0411 and the horizon's op.
  For example, with R's changes C1 at op 5, C2 at op 10 and C3 at op 20, once the cursor has
  examined C3 (`horizon_op` 20), C1 and C2 are gone: a read as of op 7 is refused, never
  answered with nothing, and a read as of op 20 answers C3.
- **Records** are the same, with the record changes track, `Record` for `Row` and
  `RecordChange` for `Change`.

### Predecessor links

The predecessor is found from links written with each object, not by a read at trim time:
- `Row.change_timestamp` and `Record.change_timestamp`: the timestamp of the row's (record's)
  latest change;
- `Change.prev_timestamp` and `RecordChange.prev_timestamp`: the previous change's timestamp, 0
  for a first change;
- `Group.version_timestamp`, and `View.version_op` and `View.version_timestamp`: the latest
  version's (`Group.op` already holds its op);
- `GroupVersion` and `ViewVersion` gain `prev_op` and `prev_timestamp`.

All are carved from reserved bytes (`Record`'s last 8 included), so no layout grows; each is a
format change, listed under "Formats".
- **The writer** sets a link from the current object, which `prefetch` already read for the
  write (the row for `row_put`, the group or view for its version). A row written twice in one
  op is updated in the cache each time, so the second change links to the first. Every write
  now updates its row, even one that leaves the same value, because `change_timestamp` moves.
- **The trim** removes the predecessor with no read: its objects-tree key is `prev_timestamp`,
  and its index key comes from the examined object's own fields (the same row's `row_key`, or
  `group_version_key(key, prev_op)` and `view_version_key(view_id, prev_op)`) with that
  timestamp.
- **Unlinked data.** An object written before R7's release has `prev_timestamp` 0 and removes
  nothing. R7's release is reached by export and import ("Formats and R6"), which rewrites every
  object with its links, so after the upgrade 0 means only a first change. A file opened in
  place keeps its unlinked history: more kept, never a wrong answer.

### The feed below the horizon

- **What it holds.** Below the horizon, the change feed is a compacted prefix: the latest change
  of each row as of the horizon, in timestamp order, much like log compaction in a message log.
  A commit key's row and its change are removed whole (see "Commit keys"), so the prefix holds no
  change of a key whose row was trimmed. That is the one exception to keep-last, and it is right
  for the prefix's purpose: the prefix rebuilds current state, where a trimmed key no longer
  exists.
- **Where the feed's horizon is.** `get_changes` reads from `timestamp_min`, not from an op, so
  it is refused by timestamp: the feed's trimmed timestamp is the larger of the changes track's
  `trimmed_ts` and the keys track's. A read is refused with `before_horizon` when that is not 0
  and `timestamp_min` is at or below it (`timestamp_min` 0, the beginning, included). Above it,
  no change at or after `timestamp_min` was removed, so the feed is complete.
  - With `keys: 7d` and `history: forever`, a reader from the beginning is told
    `before_horizon` once any key has been trimmed; it is never served a feed with key changes
    silently missing.
- **Skipping keys.** A new `ChangesFilter` flag, `skip_keys`, leaves key-table changes out of
  the reply and compares `timestamp_min` with the changes track's `trimmed_ts` only. The gateway
  sets it, since `/v1/changes` already leaves key rows out, so a key horizon alone never refuses
  `/v1/changes`.
- **The opt-in.** A second `ChangesFilter` flag, `compacted`, serves the compacted prefix instead
  of refusing.
  - `nerva view` (N35) and `nerva export` set it. Their consumers rebuild current state, which
    the prefix gives them exactly.
  - A consumer that needs every change does not set it, and is told.
  - Owner question 3.
- **The record feed**, where one is read, follows the same rule with the record changes track.

### View and group versions

- **The same keep-last rule, by predecessor link**, applied per group in GroupVersion (trees 12
  and 13) and per view in ViewVersion (trees 14 and 15) with `versions_s`: on examining a version
  V', the track removes V' 's predecessor through its `prev_op` and `prev_timestamp`. The
  invariant and its proofs are history's, with "group" or "view" for "row". Groups' and members'
  current state is untouched.
- **Reads.** A view read as of an op below the versions kind's `horizon_op` answers
  `before_horizon` (a flag bit in the view record); at or above it, it is exact.

### Commit keys: `key_expired`

- **What expires.** A key's row and its one change are removed together once the row's
  timestamp is at or below `now − keys_s`: four tombstones, in trees 1 and 2 (the row and its
  `key` index) and trees 3 and 4 (the change and its `row_key` index), all from the row's own
  fields and timestamp. The keys track walks tree 1 and skips rows of other tables; a skipped
  row counts against the pulse's budget for tree 1 but removes nothing. `nerva.zig:115`'s
  "Rows are never physically removed" becomes "except commit keys below the key horizon", and
  N3's framing changes with it.
- **The danger.** A resend of a trimmed key would find no row and commit again: the double apply
  P123 exists to prevent.
- **The key's age.** To refuse such a resend, the state machine must know the key's age without
  its row. **Keys must be UUIDv7** once a key horizon is declared (and for ever after any key was
  trimmed), and the state machine reads the key's 48-bit millisecond time (owner question 2).
  The time is part of the key: a resend of the same key carries the same time, whatever the
  client recomputes. No wire format changes; the key entry's bytes are read differently.
- **Units.** Everything below is in nanoseconds, as timestamps are. A key's time is
  `k = key_ms × 1,000,000`, the floor of its real time; both checks below use the same `k`, so the
  floor costs nothing.
- **Row timestamps sit below the prepare timestamp.** A key row is written with its entry's own
  timestamp (`commit_key_put(op, entry_timestamp, …)`), which counts up to the op's prepare
  timestamp P: `P − span_max ≤ T ≤ P`. `span_max` is the most timestamps one op reserves
  (`prepare_delta` plus `versions_reserve`, `state_machine.zig:1236`: at most
  `commit_entries_max × entry_timestamps` plus the versions reserve), a comptime constant.
  Slice 5 derives it, and asserts the bound in `commit_key_put`.
- **The order, as P123 has it.** The key's row is looked up first, before every other rule. A row
  found answers as today: `committed_before`, `key_reused` or `key_collision` (and the gateway's
  `200 {"status": "committed", "op", "replayed": true}` for the first). Only when **no row** is
  found does the age check run.
- **The age check.** Let `m = skew_max + span_max`. A keyed transaction with no row is accepted
  only if all of these hold (`now` is P):
  - `k ≤ now + skew_max` (not from the future);
  - if a key horizon is declared (`keys_s ≠ 0`): `k > now − keys_s + m`;
  - if any key was ever trimmed (the keys track's `trimmed_ts ≠ 0`): `k > trimmed_ts + m`,
    strictly.

  If it is not:
  - a key that fails the second or third bound answers `key_expired` (EntryStatus 25,
    NRV-E0213, wire `key_expired`);
  - a key that is not v7 while v7 is required, or fails the first bound, answers
    `key_time_invalid` (26, NRV-E0214).
- **Why it is safe.** A key accepted with time k, at prepare timestamp P, has
  `k ≤ P + skew_max`, and its row has `T ≥ P − span_max ≥ k − m`. Every key row trimmed has
  `T ≤ trimmed_ts`. A resend with no row that passes the third bound has `k > trimmed_ts + m`,
  so if it had ever been accepted, its row would have `T ≥ k − m > trimmed_ts` and could not
  have been trimmed. So no row means it never committed, and committing it now is its first
  commit. The bound is strict because `trimmed_ts` is itself a trimmed row's timestamp: with
  `≥`, a resend of the row at exactly `trimmed_ts` would pass and commit twice.
- **The check outlives the horizon.** The third bound, and the v7 requirement, hold whenever keys
  `trimmed_ts ≠ 0`, whatever the current declaration. Redeclaring `keys` as forever after keys
  were trimmed stops trimming, but a resend of a trimmed key still answers `key_expired`. Only a
  cluster that has never trimmed a key accepts any 16 bytes, as today. `trimmed_ts` travels in
  export v3's header, so an imported cluster keeps the check.
- **Determinism.** The check uses only the prepare timestamp, the key's bytes and the retention
  groove.
- **Who is affected.** Documents' keys (P180) follow the same rule, through `api.key_reply`. The
  Jepsen bank's checkers (P239c and P239d) must count `key_expired` as a definite refusal of a
  resend whose original outcome is known from the history, never as a loss.

### Export, import and backup

- **Export version 3.** The header carries the retention declaration and each track's cursor
  fields and `trimmed_ts`. The body is the compacted prefix followed by the ops above the
  horizon.
- **Import.** It accepts a first op above 1, and a row whose first change below the horizon is
  its latest, including a tombstone, without N81's "delete of a non-live row" refusal for that
  prefix. Ops above the horizon are replayed under every rule, as today. Every object it writes
  gets its predecessor links.
- **The key check survives import.** Import sets each track's fields from the header before it
  replays anything, so a resend of a key trimmed before the export still answers `key_expired`
  on the new cluster.
- **What import refuses.** A v3 file whose prefix is not in timestamp order, which carries a
  change above its own declared horizon in the prefix, or which carries a change of the key table
  (`0xFFFF0003`) at or below its own keys `trimmed_ts` (NRV-E0811).
- **Old files.** Export v1 and v2 files import unchanged.
- **Backups.** The manifest's version moves with export v3 (P149d).

## Risks

1. **The vendored removal path is untested upstream.** Upstream's own comment says nothing calls
   `remove`. A bug there loses or resurrects objects silently. Slice 2's fuzzer is the guard, and
   it comes before any trimming.
2. **A wrong link removes the wrong object.** Trimming trusts the predecessor links instead of
   reading, so a writer that sets a stale link (a row written twice in one op, a group created
   and changed in the same op, an import) removes a change that is still the latest, silently.
   The guards: slice 1 tests every writer's link; slice 3's model checks keep-last after every
   pulse; the VOPR's model (slice 7) predicts every as-of read; and a removal asserts that the
   predecessor's timestamp is below the examined object's and not 0. The links cost no reads
   and keep a pulse's prefetch to one scan per track.
3. **Import trusts the prefix.** Below the horizon, import replays without the ledger's pair rule
   (the pairs are gone). A forged v3 file can import an unbalanced ledger. The export's checksum
   and E0811 cover damage, not forgery, as today for v2.
4. **UUIDv7 is a new client obligation** once a key horizon is declared, and stays one once a key
   was trimmed. Nothing in `src/nerva/` names UUIDv7 at `c886c9f`: clients choose their keys
   today. Slice 5 audits every key Nerva's own tools and tests make (the gateway's retries,
   `tests/jepsen`, the demos) and makes them v7. The first client is not audited here (owner
   question 2 says what it must store).
5. **Clock skew between the client and the cluster** beyond `skew_max` turns into refusals
   (E0214), not into wrong answers. The README says so.
6. **Compaction load.** Trimming writes one tombstone per removed object, which roughly doubles
   compaction input on the trimmed trees in steady state. The demo's flat measures include it; R3's
   sizing table should be redone after R7.
7. **The digest moves for one release**, so v0.8 to v0.9 is an export and import. It happens
   while rolling upgrades are new, and the release notes must say it plainly.
8. **Growth is bounded by the live set, not absolutely**, and under overload by the backlog
   too. A load that creates rows or records still grows, by each one and its last change
   ("Goal"); many writers at the batch limit outrun trimming until the load falls (owner
   question 9).

## Owner questions

1. **R7 waits for R6 to be delivered** (`demo_r6` green on `main`), or starts slice 1 and 2 beside
   it as P269 did for R6 beside R1? **I'd wait**: slice 1 moves the digest that R6's admission
   reads, and slice 2 patches a vendored file R6 does not touch, but both are easier to review once
   R6's rules have run in CI. Slice 2 alone could run early if R6 stalls.
2. **A key's age comes from UUIDv7**, required once a key horizon is declared (and for ever
   after any key was trimmed), or from an explicit `sent_at` field in the keyed entry? Either
   changes P123 in substance: P123 says "clients choose keys; a key names one logical
   operation".
   - **UUIDv7** binds the time to the key's identity: a resend of the same key necessarily
     carries the same time, and nothing on the wire changes. The first client keeps most of its
     determinism: it derives the 74 random bits from the operation, as today, and stores only
     the 48-bit time of the first attempt (or the whole key) before its first send, then reads
     it back on every retry.
   - **`sent_at`** carries the first attempt's time beside the key, in the key entry's value
     bytes after the key, which must be zero today (`key_invalid`, 22). The key stays the
     client's choice. But a resend whose key is stored and whose `sent_at` is recomputed (the
     natural bug) passes the age check once the row is trimmed, and commits twice;
     `commit_key_checksum` does not cover the key entry, and once the row is gone nothing is
     left to compare with. If chosen, Formats gains "the key entry's value bytes after the key
     (a wire format)", which is a stop until approved; E0214 becomes "`sent_at` missing or
     ahead by more than `skew_max`"; slice 5 reads `sent_at` instead of the key's bits, and
     the checksum is extended to cover it.
   - **Neither** works for a client that cannot store anything before its first send. A key
     horizon is then unsafe for it, and it should not be declared.

   **UUIDv7**: no wire change, and the time cannot drift from the key. Either way the key
   horizon is opt-in (question 5), and the README says what a client must store.
3. **The feed below the horizon** is refused by default (`before_horizon`), with an opt-in to the
   compacted prefix for `nerva view` and export; or is it served compacted to everyone, silently?
   **Refused by default**: a consumer that counts changes would otherwise be wrong without knowing.
4. **The demo's time scale.** A 60 s horizon over 30 horizons (about 35 minutes, real cluster), or
   a VOPR-only demo at 1 simulated day × 30 (fast, but not a real cluster)? **60 s × 30 on a real
   cluster**, with the VOPR drawing horizons per seed beside it; the PRODUCTION.md wording "at
   simulated time" is amended in `docs/demos/r7.md`.
5. **The default horizon is forever** (nothing trimmed unless declared), or a default such as 30
   days for keys? **Forever**: a new default would drop data from clusters that never asked, and
   would change behaviour across the export and import into R7's release.
6. **`retention_min` 60 s and `skew_max` 10 s**, or larger (for example 1 hour and 60 s) for
   production safety? **60 s and 10 s**: the demo needs 60 s, and a larger minimum belongs in the
   operator's declaration, not the bound. The README recommends days.
7. **Trimming by pulses instead of compaction.** `docs/PRODUCTION.md` scopes R7 as "Compaction
   drops what is older, deterministically". This brief has pulses write tombstones and leaves
   compaction only to reclaim them, because compaction never rewrites the append-only trees
   ("Non-goals"). Accept the change, and amend PRODUCTION.md's scope line in the answer's pull
   request? **Yes**: the goal (bounded, deterministic, in the log) is kept; only the mechanism
   moves. This brief does not edit that line.
8. **Facts.** GOALS-1.0 goal 7 names "retention of keys and facts", and PRODUCTION.md's goal is
   "disk use, export and import stay bounded". As designed, a load that creates rows or records,
   like the first client's facts (P123b), grows by each one and its last change. Options:
   - (a) cut facts' retention from R7 to a later milestone, and amend GOALS-1.0's line and
     PRODUCTION.md's goal to "bounded by the live set plus one horizon" for R7;
   - (b) add expiry of rows or records by age to R7, a new kind with its own reads and refusals;
   - (c) leave it to the application, which deletes what it no longer needs. This does not bound
     facts either: tombstones are kept (N3), so a deleted record still holds its 512 bytes and
     its last change;
   - (d) reclaim tombstones, and the deleted row's last change, below the history horizon. That
     amends N3, and with (c) bounds a load that deletes.

   **(a)**, as this brief is written: R7 ships keys, history and versions, and (b) or (c) with
   (d) is the later milestone. Expiring live facts by age is a semantic change no client has
   asked for. The demo's step 7 measures the growth, so the answer can be checked against
   numbers.
9. **The lag bound** ("The lag bound"). Trimming keeps pace with one writer, and with q writers
   while they write on average at most L/q objects per tree per op; under heavier load the
   backlog grows on disk and drains afterwards. Options and what R7 ships under each:
   - (a) no change: R7 ships the stated bound, W0704 as the alarm, demo step 8 asserting the
     drain, and README stating it;
   - (b) deterministic backpressure: R7 adds status 27 `retention_behind` and its code (a wire
     change in slice 1), a hard backlog bound of `lag_max` of writes plus `clients_max` × L,
     and refusals under overload, asserted in step 8;
   - (c) a vendored patch to `replica.zig` putting the pulse at the head of the request queue:
     a named stop item until approved, recorded in UPSTREAM.md, which still allows 8 client ops
     between pulses, so R7 ships (a)'s bound with q ≤ 8.

   **(a)**: the backlog is disk, not memory, and drains; nothing unbounded is held in memory;
   one writer, the first client's shape today, always keeps pace; and it needs neither a wire
   change nor a vendored patch. (b) is the upgrade if the demo's drain is too slow.
