# R5 demo: recover

**Status: demonstrated.** `demo_r5` passed in CI on `main` at `a25fb19` (the merge of #161), 2026-11-05: [the run](https://github.com/NervaLog/nerva/actions/runs/41997293568/job/127143488126). R5's brief is [docs/briefs/r5.md](../briefs/r5.md).

- **The demo:** `zig build demo-r5`, which runs `tests/demo/r5.py` against a ReleaseSafe
  `nerva`, and the `demo_r5` job in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` (slice 7).
- **What it shows:** a replica whose data file is deleted is formatted again with
  `nerva recover` and catches up while writes continue; it then leads a view, and every view
  hash, the ledger's total and every acknowledged commit key agree. `nerva backup`, a restore
  drill beside the running cluster, a wipe and `nerva restore` give the same hashes. The brief's
  table, "The demo", lists every step and its expected outcome.

This document grows slice by slice: what each slice ran, the cuts (with the reason), and the
recorded output of the green run.

## Slice 4: recover on imported clusters

Run on 2026-11-05 in the cloud container, ReleaseSafe (not CI):

- `tests/release/recover.py` (in `test:release`): a formatted cluster commits and is exported,
  three replicas are imported from the export, a writer runs past a checkpoint; a replica
  recovered with `--origin=none` starts, exits 8 with NRV-E0711 naming the export, and its
  peers log that they ignore it and commit on; then each replica in turn (the primary once) is
  lost and recovered with the export's SHA-256, and with another backup stopped the commits need
  it; last, the backup that would lead the next view stops and the primary is killed with
  SIGKILL, so the recovered replica is in the view-change quorum; every acknowledged key answers
  `replayed`. About 80 to 125 s.
- The VOPR's end-of-run summary prints `reformats=<n> origin=none|non-zero`. Only about one seed
  in seventy has both a reformat and a non-zero origin (reformats happen on swarm seeds that
  crash and restart replicas); the seeds named in the PR were found by scanning.
- Cut: the VOPR still starts every cluster at op 0; reformats on an imported start are slice 5.

## Slice 6: `nerva backup` and `nerva restore`

Ran: the unit tests of `src/nerva/backup.zig` and `tests/release/restore.py` (part of
`zig build test:release`), against a real three-replica cluster under writes. Not `demo-r5`.

- A backup `--as-of` an earlier op while a writer commits; its manifest equals the file's size
  and SHA-256 and the cluster's own view hash, group count and ledger total as of that op.
- A restore drill into a one-replica cluster with another cluster id while the first keeps
  committing, then the first cluster stopped and wiped and its three replicas restored: every
  view hash and ledger total read back equals the manifest's.
- Refusals, one coded line each and no data file: a backup with one byte changed (NRV-E0804),
  another backup's manifest, a manifest with a changed view hash or ledger total (NRV-E0809), a
  missing manifest and six malformed ones (NRV-E0808); and for `backup`, an existing output or
  manifest (NRV-E0004) and an op the cluster has not reached (NRV-E0802).

Cuts: none. The manifest is not covered by a checksum of its own (the brief's format has none);
a flipped byte in a view hash or a total is caught by NRV-E0809, one in the release or the
cluster id (not compared, by design) is not.

## Slice 5: the VOPR from an imported start

Ran: `zig build check`, `test:fmt`, `test:unit`, `test:release`, and VOPR seeds (ReleaseSafe,
`nerva` lane). Not `demo-r5`.

- The `nerva` lane draws per seed (half of them) whether the cluster starts imported: one
  deterministic export, built from the seed in memory, is restored into every replica's simulated
  storage with `import.zig`'s importer after the format, so the seed starts at checkpoint 19, 39
  or 59 with empty journals. The state checker starts at that checkpoint; reformatted replicas
  take the export's origin and state-sync as before.
- Cut: the export holds no changes, views or ledgers (only its op, so only the importer's own
  versions and checkpoints), because the workload's model starts empty. Importing rows needs the
  model to start with them; left for after R5.
- Seeds 1 and 5 do not draw an imported start (2 and 200 do); the lane's draw is by the cluster
  seed. Results are in the slice's report.

How each step is checked, and where it differs from the brief's table:

- **The writer** (steps 1 to 8) sends one keyed transaction after another: a seat put and two
  ledger pairs (fund from a source to an account's `available`, reserve `available` to `held`),
  so the ledger's total stays 0. A 503, a 504 or a lost connection is resent under the same key;
  anything else fails the demo. Over the run it was never handed a 503 (`0 resent`): the gateway
  absorbed the primary's changes. That the resend path answers `committed` or `replayed` is
  exercised by step 8's replay of every key, and by `tests/recover/recover.py`.
- **Gauges.** `replica_commit_min` and `replica_view` are the brief's names and the code's
  (`replica.zig:4091`, `:4095`); on the wire they are `tb.replica_view:N|g|#cluster:..,replica:R`,
  sent every 10 seconds, which the script's UDP socket parses. Step 6 waits for a sample of each
  replica taken after `sync: done` and for replica 2's sample, taken after the others', to reach
  the highest of theirs. Step 7 reads `replica_view` from samples newer than the last restart.
- **Step 4** uses a path that does not exist (`1_probe.nerva`), because a path that exists is
  refused first (NRV-E0005); the refusal is NRV-E0713 and neither the path nor `.recovering`
  exists afterwards.
- **Step 5's `recover:` line** is a log line on stderr (`info(recover): recover: replica 2 ...`);
  the script requires exactly one, the view two past the cluster's.
- **Step 8** compares, at the writer's last acknowledged op, `nerva api`'s and `nerva view`'s
  groups and `hash` byte for byte. `nerva view` serves no `view_hash` (only `nerva api` does), so
  the replicated `view_hash` is checked by recomputing it in the script from `nerva view`'s
  groups, as `tests/views/maintained.py` does, and by step 9's manifest and the restores. Every
  acknowledged key is resent and must answer `replayed` with its original op.
- **Step 9's op** is higher than step 8's: each replayed key in step 8 is an op of its own. The
  views and the ledger did not change, and the manifest equals step 8's hashes and total.
- **Step 12** flips the middle byte of a copy of the backup; its CRC breaks first, so the refusal
  is NRV-E0804 (the brief allows it), not NRV-E0809. NRV-E0809 is covered by
  `tests/release/restore.py`.
- Step 11 also starts `nerva view` on the restored cluster and compares it with `nerva api`, and
  commits once to show the restored cluster takes writes.

Earlier local runs while writing the script stopped at step 5 (it looked for the `recover:` line
without its log prefix) and at step 6 (it read the others' gauges before they had arrived); both
were the script's, not the product's, and were fixed before the run above. Nothing was re-run to
make a product failure pass.

Cuts: none.
