**v0.7.1 is tagged before the 24-hour soak of `c886c9f` ends, and with #372 open.**

The owner chose this in session on 2026-11-14 ("let's tag it"), waiving the rest of P147b's
24 hours of fleet rounds on the candidate `c886c9f`, which P388 restarted. The owner also accepts #372 (SHOULD: `nerva format` blames
the wrong layer for an unwritable or missing directory) as a known issue of v0.7.1; its fix is
#395, after the tag.

Why: the pinned code of `c886c9f` (`src/`, `build.zig`, `build.zig.zon`, `zig/`, `.gitmodules`,
`.gitattributes`) is identical to `5d7d806`'s, and the fleet ran `5d7d806` for 15.0 hours on
2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13 (tracker #177), and ran 74,305 seeds with no failure as
counted by `5d7d806`'s own loop (see below).

Six of those seeds ran past their per-seed deadline (2 `vopr-nerva` on 2026-11-12; 3
`vopr-nerva` and 1 `vopr-testing` on 2026-11-13), which P287b counts as not clean. The owner
waives them as well (in session, 2026-11-14). They cannot be replayed: the fleet draws seeds from
`/dev/urandom`, `sim_fleet.py` keeps only a count of past-deadline seeds per target, and `run.sh`
deletes a round's results once they are counted. So the condition is (owner, in session,
2026-11-14): after the tag the fleet is changed to keep the seed number of every seed past its
deadline, as it does for a failing one, and every such seed it then records on `c886c9f` or later
is replayed with a long deadline (#452, open);
one that never finishes is a liveness bug for a v0.7.2. The six stay unexplained. The soak restarted because
`5d7d806`'s own fleet loop could lose a failing seed (a refused round, #370; a worker that died in
its failure path, #387; both fixed by #382), not because the shipped code changed. The 15 hours were
counted by that loop, and the host's journal and `last-failed-round` were not checked for a refused round; a dead
worker leaves no record in the round or the state file at `5d7d806`, at most the failing
command's own message in the journal. Both
release reviews and P369's short review found no blocker left open; Jepsen is P388's.

The verdicts the release notes quote are as each reviewer's session reported them to the
release's supervisor session, and are recorded nowhere else: the first reviewer (Fable, P369),
"ship after MUSTs: one MUST, about the release process, not the code" (#371, fixed by #374);
the second, "ship v0.7.1, no MUSTs"; P369's short review, "ship once #384 is fixed or waived"
(#384, fixed by #386). The findings themselves are the `release-review` issues.

What it costs: the 15 hours are weaker evidence than 15 hours on `c886c9f`, since the loop that
counted them could not see a refused round or a dead worker. The release PR quotes no `sim_fleet.py soak` line of 24.0 hours on the candidate.
The fleet's loop at `c886c9f` ran for less than a day before the tag (the tracker reports no
hours on it, and its own `sim_fleet.py soak` line was not read), so a fault it would only
have shown on that commit, rather than on `5d7d806`, is found after the release, if at all. The
fleet keeps running `c886c9f` until the soak pin is removed; a failure it files against it goes to
the retro and a v0.7.2. Operators read #372 under Known issues.
